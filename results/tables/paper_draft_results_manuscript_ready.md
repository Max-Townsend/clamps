## Figure 2

## Figure 3
A 2 (phase: baseline vs late adaptation) x 10 (assigned clamp group) x 2 (input device) mixed ANOVA on participant-averaged mean hand angle (N = 484) showed a main effect of phase, F(1, 464) = 288.53, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .383, 95% CI [.298, .513], a phase x group interaction, F(9, 464) = 11.31, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .180, 95% CI [.130, .306], and a phase x device interaction, F(1, 464) = 12.75, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .027, 95% CI [.008, .065], but no phase x group x device interaction, F(9, 464) = 1.69, p = .088, eta_p^2 = .032, 95% CI [.020, .085]. Holm-corrected paired t-tests were significant at 26 of 40 clamp magnitudes (all p_Holms <= .049), but not at any of the 8 magnitudes in the 135-170 deg range (all p_Holms = 1.000). In the peak range, late adaptation exceeded baseline by 27.49 deg, t(98) = 12.45, p < .001, cohens_dz = 1.251, 95% CI [.872, 1.770]. By contrast, the phase difference was negligible in the 135-170 deg range, t(96) = 0.27, p = .789, cohens_dz = .027, 95% CI [-.166, .243].

A corresponding mixed ANOVA on between-participant spread of mean hand angle, quantified as absolute deviation from the phase-specific group median (N = 484), showed a main effect of phase, F(1, 464) = 167.74, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .266, 95% CI [.198, .385], but no phase x group interaction, F(9, 464) = 1.74, p = .077, eta_p^2 = .033, 95% CI [.025, .101], no phase x device interaction, F(1, 464) = 1.99, p = .158, eta_p^2 = .004, 95% CI [.000, .022], and no phase x group x device interaction, F(9, 464) = 1.11, p = .354, eta_p^2 = .021, 95% CI [.013, .069]. Holm-corrected paired tests on spread were significant at 25 of 40 clamp magnitudes (all p_Holms <= .025), including 7 of the 8 magnitudes in the 135-170 deg range (significant tests: all p_Holms <= .011; remaining test: p_Holm = .542).

Within the 135-170 deg clamp range, bias direction predicted late adaptation, Wald chi2(2, N = 96 participants) = 33.18, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .165, 95% CI [.073, .290]. Relative to the near-zero group, the Incorrect group showed less late adaptation (-6.38 deg, 95% CI [-10.07, -2.70], p_Holm < .001), whereas the Correct group showed more late adaptation (7.62 deg, 95% CI [3.99, 11.26], p_Holm < .001).

## Figure 4
Across each participant's assigned clamp magnitudes, late adaptation was higher for Mouse than Trackpad (Mouse mean = 18.25 deg, 95% CI [15.67, 20.83]; Trackpad mean = 13.16 deg, 95% CI [10.56, 15.76]), Welch t(467.87) = 2.74, p = .006, g = .246, 95% CI [.063, .433]. A clamp-adjusted clustered model gave a Mouse-Trackpad difference of 5.93 deg, 95% CI [2.79, 9.07], p < .001. This device difference was particularly clear in the peak range (15-50 deg), where Mouse participants showed greater adaptation than Trackpad participants (Mouse mean = 35.41 deg, 95% CI [29.81, 41.00]; Trackpad mean = 21.55 deg, 95% CI [15.87, 27.22]), Welch t(96.94) = 3.50, p < .001, g = .697, 95% CI [.322, 1.150]; the corresponding clamp-adjusted Mouse-Trackpad difference was 13.94 deg, 95% CI [5.91, 21.98], p < .001.

Task-aligned baseline bias did not differ by device (Mouse mean = -0.08 deg, 95% CI [-1.05, 0.88]; Trackpad mean = -0.09 deg, 95% CI [-1.10, 0.91]), Welch t(457.03) = 0.01, p = .988, g = .001, 95% CI [-.179, .185]. The raw signed counterpart, computed from unflipped baseline hand angles without clamp-sign task alignment, was Mouse mean = -4.28 deg, 95% CI [-5.11, -3.46], and Trackpad mean = 1.23 deg, 95% CI [0.23, 2.22], Welch t(423.50) = -8.43, p < .001, g = -.781, 95% CI [-.977, -.590]. Spatial bias patterns differed by device across targets when tested in the task-aligned frame, Wald chi2(3, N = 480 participants) = 2.10, p = .553, eta_p^2 = .001, 95% CI [.000, .007], and in the raw signed frame, Wald chi2(3, N = 480 participants) = 117.92, p < .001, eta_p^2 = .054, 95% CI [.036, .077].

Because participant-level reach-variability estimates were strictly positive and strongly right-skewed, device differences in variability were summarised with medians and IQRs and tested with clamp-adjusted log-scale models, with rank-based tests reported as robustness checks. Early reach variability was higher for Mouse than Trackpad (Mouse median = 14.31 deg, IQR = 9.95-24.27, 95% CI [13.37, 15.89]; Trackpad median = 9.82 deg, IQR = 7.51-18.33, 95% CI [9.17, 11.53]), Mann-Whitney U = 35683, p < .001, rank-biserial = .253, 95% CI [.148, .355]. In the clamp-adjusted log-scale model, Mouse variability was 1.33 times higher than Trackpad, 95% CI [1.16, 1.52], p < .001. Late reach variability showed the same direction (Mouse median = 12.21 deg, IQR = 8.20-23.90, 95% CI [11.24, 14.22]; Trackpad median = 9.09 deg, IQR = 6.29-15.50, 95% CI [8.17, 9.98]), Mann-Whitney U = 35478, p < .001, rank-biserial = .246, 95% CI [.138, .348]. In the clamp-adjusted log-scale model, Mouse variability was 1.37 times higher than Trackpad, 95% CI [1.20, 1.55], p < .001.

## Methods Wording For Reach Variability
For device comparisons in reach variability, participant-level SD measures were strictly positive and strongly right-skewed, so raw-scale mean comparisons were sensitive to a small number of high-variability observations. Device effects were therefore tested with clamp-adjusted linear models on log-transformed variability values using participant-clustered robust standard errors, and are reported as Mouse/Trackpad ratios with 95% confidence intervals. Medians, IQRs, and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals are reported descriptively, with Mann-Whitney tests and rank-biserial effect sizes provided as robustness checks.
